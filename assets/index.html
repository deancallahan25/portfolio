About me

Connecting technical methods with real-world problems.

My academic interests lie at the intersection of data science, applied mathematics, economics, and environmental research. I am interested in how quantitative tools can help us better understand the systems that shape communities and the environment.

Through research experiences and interdisciplinary projects, I have worked with environmental datasets, statistical methods, predictive modeling, geospatial analysis, and data integration. These experiences have strengthened my interest in building analytical approaches that are both technically rigorous and useful for decision-making.

My long-term goal is to contribute to research that connects computational methods with environmental sustainability, economic opportunity, and evidence-based public policy.

My approach
Tools age. Reasoning does not.

I value reproducible analysis, careful interpretation, transparent assumptions, and asking the right questions before choosing the right model.
